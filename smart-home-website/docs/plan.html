The read

You are selling into a market that is growing fast and buying nervously. Those two facts should decide almost everything about the website.

India's smart home market is put at roughly USD 6.7 billion in 2026, compounding near 29% a year toward USD 24 billion by 2031. Demand is not the problem. The problem is that a homeowner deciding between you and the next installer is making a one-to-three lakh decision about wiring they cannot see, on a product category where they have heard at least one horror story.

Published benchmarks put a core setup — touch switch boards, a lock, curtains, a video door phone — at about ₹80,000 for a 1BHK, ₹1.3 lakh for a 2BHK, ₹1.8 lakh for a 3BHK, with full-villa work running ₹3–5 lakh. Your prices will differ. What matters is that these numbers are already public, so a site that refuses to talk about price reads as evasive, not premium.

What buyers are actually afraid of

Survey work on smart home hesitancy is consistent, and none of the top objections are "I don't want this." They are all risk. Every one of them is answerable on a web page, and most competitor sites answer none of them.

This table is the real content brief. If the site does nothing else, it should retire these five worries in order.

The position

Nobody wants a smart switch. They want the house to already know it's evening.

Every competitor site in this category sells a parts list: switches, curtains, locks, sensors, a grid of product photos and a contact form. It is the easiest positioning to copy and the hardest to win on, because a parts list can only be beaten on price.

You already have the better story sitting in your service list and are underselling it. Room settings and environment settings are not two more line items — they are the actual product. The switches are how it gets built. The scene is what gets bought.

So the site sells outcomes and prices them as packages: Good Morning opens the curtains and warms the lights. Movie drops them to 20% and closes everything. Leaving kills every load in the house from the door. That framing does three useful things at once — it makes you memorable, it makes price comparison harder, and it gives every page a reason to show motion instead of a product shot.

New revenue · recommended

Annual maintenance contract

The industry benchmark for a service contract is 5–8% of the installed value per year, and integrators running preventive checks with remote monitoring report callbacks down by up to half. On a ₹1.5 lakh install that is ₹8–12k a year of revenue you are currently doing for free as goodwill. It also turns project income into recurring income, which is what makes the business worth something if you ever sell it.

New revenue

The designer and builder channel

Interior designers and small builders specify automation before the client ever searches for it, and they specify whoever makes them look good. A single "For architects & interior designers" page — with a spec sheet, conduit and back-box requirements, and a stated timeline for coordinating with their electrician — is the cheapest page on this site and probably the highest earning.

New revenue

Retrofit audit

A paid, credited-back home visit: what your existing wiring will and won't allow, what it would cost, what to do first. Charging a small fee filters tyre-kickers and converts far better than a free quote, because the customer has already bought something once.

New revenue

Beyond the home

The same skills sell to serviced apartments, boutique hotels, clinics and small offices at higher ticket sizes and with a natural AMC attached. One page each, later — but design the site so the categories can be added without a rebuild.

How it converts

A beautiful site that ends in a contact form is a brochure. Six mechanics turn it into a pipeline, listed in the order I would build them.

Highest value

WhatsApp as the primary path

In India this is not a nice-to-have. A persistent WhatsApp button pre-filled with the page context ("Hi, I'm looking at smart curtains for a 3BHK in —") will out-convert every form on the site. Keep the form for people who prefer it, but stop making it the main door.

Highest value

The estimator

Pick your home size, tick the rooms, tick the services, get an honest range and a PDF by email. It answers the number-one objection, it qualifies the lead before you spend a site visit on them, and the drop-off point tells you exactly which service scares people.

Differentiator

The live scene demo

An interactive room on the homepage where the visitor taps a scene and watches the lights dim and the curtains move. Nobody in this market does it. It is the product, demonstrated, in five seconds — and it is the one thing that will get the site remembered and shared. A prototype is below.

Trust

Real projects with real numbers

Six homes, photographed properly, each with the brief, the kit installed, the timeline and the actual spend. One honest project page with a price on it beats twenty stock photos.

Traffic

Cost-guide content

"Home automation cost in India", "smart switches vs retrofit modules", "do smart curtains work with existing tracks". These are the queries with buying intent and they are winnable — each one funnels into the estimator.

Traffic

Google Business Profile, worked weekly

For a service-area business the profile often outperforms the website for calls. Photos of finished jobs, weekly posts, and a deliberate review routine after every handover. Expect four to eight weeks before rankings shift.

What the design research says

I worked through the smart home design canon — Dribbble's home automation, smart home UI, smart home website and smart home dashboard tags run to a few thousand shots between them, with named pieces like Hommatic, AuraSphere, CUE Home Automation and Enver Studio's smart home hero doing the rounds — plus this year's broader web trends.

The visual consensus is remarkably tight, and worth stating plainly: near-black grounds, glassmorphic translucent cards, one saturated accent, generous rounding, a floating room-card dashboard, weather and temperature readouts, and a hero photograph of an expensive living room at dusk.

Here is the trap. Almost every one of those shots is a product — an app dashboard for software that already exists, designed for someone who has already bought. You are a services business selling an installation. If we copy the canon straight, we get a gorgeous interface for a product you don't ship, and the pages that actually close deals — price, process, proof, warranty — end up as an afterthought in 12px grey on black.

So: borrow the language, reject the structure. Take the scene chips, the room cards, the dimming and the dark cinematic photography. Build them on top of a high-trust services architecture where price and process are first-class citizens. From this year's wider trends, the two worth using are bento grids for the service overview and scroll-driven reveal for the process page; the engagement figures attached to them come from agency marketing and should be read as directional, not as evidence.

Dribbble itself is blocked from direct access on this machine, so the shot-level detail above comes from search indexing rather than my opening each piece. The pattern reading is confident; if you want specific shots annotated, send me links or screenshots and I'll mark them up.

Build

On stack: this should be a static site — Astro or Next in static-export mode, on Vercel or Netlify, content in files or a light CMS if you want to edit copy yourself. No WordPress. The pages are mostly fixed, the estimator is a small piece of client-side JavaScript posting to one endpoint, and a static build gives you the load speed that both mobile visitors and Google actually reward. Images through an image CDN, because project photography is what will otherwise make this site slow.

Ongoing

What I need from you

Nothing is built yet. These seven answers unblock the whole thing, and the first three are the ones that change the design.

Which direction?

Switchplate is my recommendation, with the Living Panel demo on top. Say the word and I build that system out properly — or pick another and I'll develop that one instead.

Brand name, logo, and do you have one already?

Everything above says YOURBRAND. If there is existing branding, send it and the palette bends to fit it rather than the other way round.

Which cities do you actually serve?

This decides the city pages and the entire local SEO plan. Only list places you will genuinely send a van to.

Real package prices

Even rough ranges. A site with honest ranges beats a site with "contact us for pricing" by a distance, and the estimator cannot exist without them.

Photography — what exists?

Finished-job photos are the single most valuable asset here. If you have none, we plan a half-day shoot at two recent installs before phase four.

Which brands do you install?

Matters for the obsolescence objection and for what we can honestly claim about Matter support and app consolidation.

Is "elixir" Alexa?

As flagged above. A one-word answer.
